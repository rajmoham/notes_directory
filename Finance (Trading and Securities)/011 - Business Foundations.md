# Banking
Confidence (and trust) is a key part of banking and banks.

A banks balance sheet has assets and liabilities.
- Assets are mainly loans that was given out and will be paid back
- Liabilities are mainly deposits of customers that have the right to their money

Maturity Transformation -> transformation from short term deposits to long term loans, this is a core activity of a bank and the main way they make money

In essence, banks "borrow" in the form of deposits and lend them out in loans for some term. They should receive the money back (and more from interest). And the trust factor is that they should have the money for those who has deposited when they need it.

Credit Intermediation -> banks are good are credit risk, and lending only to people who they believe can pay them back

Bank Run -> when large number of customers simultaneously withdraw their deposits when they panic over a bank's impending failure. Banks always do not have enough money to pay everyone at once.

## Money
1. Commercial bank money -> deposits, checking account, current accounts
2. Central bank money -> funds held by banks on deposit at the central bank
3. Currency in circulation -> notes and coins

Credit creation -> where most of the money (commercial money) in the economy is created by the banks, and in process of lending money

## Central Banks
Essentially a bank for banks, owned by the government, country or some supranational entity.

Main functions:
- Control inflation
- Maintain stability in financial system
- Encourage economic growth for some countries
- Some of the activities done will also create central bank money

## Central Bank Money
When a bank does not have enough money to meet the liabilities of a deposit, then a central bank can provide a unsecured loan. This lending "creates" central bank money. And similarly, the central bank has a loan as an asset and the credit to the bank as a liability.

Central Bank Repo -> central bank receive collateral (reserve repo) and credits account of commercial bank.
To support the economy, high quality collateral like government bonds are bought from commercial banks. Money is paid by simply crediting the reserve account of the bank. (Quantitative Easing)

## Cash
Central bank issues cash. This is similar to crediting the economy and therefore they have the liability.
This is done by the banks requesting some sum of money and the amount if added to the reserve account of the bank.

# How to move money
### Moving money within a bank
When two parties are within the same bank, no money moves around. Since the bank owns both accounts, money is credited and debited. Only the numbers in the account changes.
### Moving money within different banks (within same country)
Bank A transfers an amount to the central bank, and then the central bank transfers the same amount to Bank B. 
This means nothing happens except numbers have moved in the central bank for Bank A and Bank B's accounts.
### Moving money internationally
Bank A will transfer money to the bank account of the FX dealer via the central bank of their country (e.g. United States). They will exchange the money from one currency to another, debiting the central bank of another country (e.g. Switzerland). Then this central bank will debit Bank B.
